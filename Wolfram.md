# Wolfram

From October 2025 to October 2026, I was a research fellow at the [Wolfram Institute](https://www.wolframinstitute.org/) under the direction of [Stephen Wolfram](https://www.stephenwolfram.com/). My fellowship was funded by the John Templeton Foundation through the grant [Causality, Observers and Computation: A New Approach to Fundamental Physics and Metamathematics][templeton-grant]. My main responsibility was to develop a dictionary of discrete geometric concepts for the [Wolfram Physics Project](https://www.wolframphysics.org/). At the Institute, we used the term *Infrageometry* for this work. [Carlos Zapata-Carratalá](https://zapatacarratala.me/) also uses the term in his [work at Arity Science](https://arity.science/findings/infrageometry/), where he explores how rewrites on hypergraphs can express geometric operations such as homotopy of paths.

After initial consultations with Stephen Wolfram and Nik Murzin, I set out to develop a physically and mathematically sound pipeline for reconstructing spacetime for a given observer from a single history in a multiway system of hypergraph rewriting. In particular, I wanted to understand how geometry might emerge when an observer measures a large graph at a coarse scale.

Before leaving the Institute, I consolidated parts of this computational research in the [InfraGeometry repository](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/InfraGeometry), whose research documentation outlines the theoretical background. This work forms part of the public deliverables under the Templeton grant. I also started several [other repositories at the Institute][manifesto].

I describe this line of research in more detail in [my Multihistory statement](https://multihistory.org/statements/#pavel-hajek) and will continue it through that collaboration.
